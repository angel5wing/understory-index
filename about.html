what is this?

a living archive of local fungi, of course!

understory index is a digital field guide to london's boundless mycological magic, bringing together community observations, photographs & ecological records through the iNaturalist API.

part field guide, part experiment in information design, the project explores how biodiversity data can be organised, interpreted & experienced beyond the conventions of a scientific database.

built using observations contributed to iNaturalist, the project brings together photographs, species identifications, observation dates & locations to reveal some of the often-overlooked organisms living right alongside us!

how does it work?

i'm so glad you asked! to keep it simple, understory index retrieves local observational data directly from the iNaturalist API using JavaScript.

each request returns structured JSON data, which is processed and presented as an individual specimen entry. visitors can explore additional observations through pagination and follow links back to the original community records.

the integration also accounts for incomplete records, repeated observations and unsuccessful requests, helping the index remain usable as new data is retrieved.

because the images belong to their respective creators, the index selects images with 'CC0' or 'CC BY' licences and maintains a dedicated credits page with photographer information, original sources and licensing details.

future integrations

understory index currently presents fungal observations as individual records, but fungi rarely exist in isolation!

future development will explore a second, relational mode of discovery, connecting observations to the wider ecological systems in which they exist. by integrating additional biodiversity and environmental datasets, the project could begin to reveal relationships between fungi, plants, habitats, substrates and the processes of decomposition and symbiosis.

the aim is to move beyond cataloguing individual organisms towards an interface that makes ecological interdependence more visible, transforming the index from a collection of observations into an interconnected, living archive.
